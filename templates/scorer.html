How the Candidate Scorer Works

For Andrew — how to train it, backtest it, and correct it when it's wrong.

Two layers, not one

The scorer is not pure kNN. It's two passes working together:

1️⃣
Rubric scoring (Gemini)
Gemini reads the candidate profile and applies the explicit SDR rubric — pedigree, location, grad year, tenure, background fit, athlete signal, message quality. Outputs a tier. Works from day one with zero prior candidates.
2️⃣
kNN calibration
Compares the new candidate to your labeled historical candidates by vector similarity. Shows who they're most like and how those people were tiered. Improves over time as you label more.
Why two layers? Pure kNN can't work until you have labeled data. The rubric gives you day-one coverage; kNN catches cases the rubric misses or where your gut differed from the written rule.

Example: Jake Liu

Dartmouth 2023 · JPMorgan Analyst · NYC · No message

  1. 1.Pre-filter: Grad year 2023 → passes. NYC → passes. No hard disqualifiers.
  2. 2.Enrichment: Gemini rewrites: "Class of 2023 Dartmouth graduate, JPMorgan analyst, NYC, IB/finance background, no message."
  3. 3.Embedding: That paragraph becomes a 3,072-number vector. Jake's point in semantic space is near other top-school/finance/NYC candidates.
  4. 4.Rubric: Top school ✓ · Brand employer ✓ · 2022+ ✓ · NYC ✓ · Finance ✓ · Message blank → neutral. Result: Strong Intro.
  5. 5.kNN: "Similar to: Sarah Chen (Strong Intro, 94%), Mike Torres (Weak Intro, 87%)" — confirms the tier.

How to train it

The rubric layer needs no training — it's ready now. The kNN layer gets better every time you label a candidate at /label.

→Each label you submit becomes a training example with a tier + embedding
→After ~20–30 labels, kNN starts providing real calibration
→After ~50 labels, it's meaningfully improving tier confidence
→Your 70 historical inbounds are the fastest way to bootstrap — import them at /import, then label them all

How to backtest it

  1. 1.Export your ~70 historical inbounds as a CSV from Airtable or Webflow
  2. 2.Upload at /import — they're scored automatically but never emailed
  3. 3.Check /candidates and compare model tiers to what you actually did with each person
  4. 4.Look for patterns in the disagreements — those reveal rubric gaps or edge cases to address
The ground-truth test: feed in the 70 historical candidates cold, see what % land on the correct tier. That's your baseline accuracy. Anything under ~70% agreement is a signal the rubric prompt needs tuning.

How to correct it when it's wrong

One-off disagreement
Go to /label and give the correct label. kNN recalibrates — future similar candidates shift toward your correction.
Systematic mis-scoring (a whole category keeps being wrong)
Edit RUBRIC_SYSTEM_PROMPT in knn_scorer.py. Example: if state-school + Ramp keeps coming up Weak Intro but you want Strong Intro, add Ramp to the feeder company list. Redeploy and the rubric applies everywhere immediately.
The message field classifier is off
Gemini classifies messages as specific, ai_slop, or neutral. If it's misreading messages, add examples to the rubric prompt and label those candidates manually to anchor kNN.

Schools and companies — do you need a list?

Gemini already knows Goldman > regional bank, Dartmouth > state school. You don't need a separate lookup table for general pedigree.
